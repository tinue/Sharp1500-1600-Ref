# PC-1600 ROM Disassembly — Pointer

> **STATUS: POINTER DOC.** Brief index into the ROM dumps and their annotated
> disassemblies, which live outside this corpus. Updated 2026-09-27.

## Where it lives

**`PC-1600-ROM/`** ([github.com/tinue/PC-1600-ROM](https://github.com/tinue/PC-1600-ROM))
holds the real-hardware ROM dumps and their annotated disassemblies:

| Path | What |
|---|---|
| `README.md` | Dump provenance, ROM versions, MD5s, how the dumps were made. |
| `dumps/{new,old}/PC1600-*.BIN` | Calculator ROM, NEW and OLD revision: P0-B0, P1-B0, P1-B3, P1-B3B, P2-B6 (Z-80) and the LH5803's own ROM (C000–FFFFH). |
| `dumps/ce1600p/{new,old}/` | CE-1600P ROM: bank 4 (printer/plotter) and bank 5 (CE-1600F floppy at 4000H, cassette at 6000H). |
| `disasm/…/<ROM>.asm` | One annotated disassembly per dump, same paths. All 16 reassemble byte-identical. |
| `disasm/README.md` | What each image contains, what changed from OLD to NEW, and how to reassemble. |

Labels carry the names used in Sharp's TRM / Systemhandbuch; the LH5803 ROM carries the
names of the PC-1500 A04 ROM it is based on. Dialects: the Z-80 images are
[`z80asm`](https://www.nongnu.org/z80asm/) source, the LH5803 image is `sdas`
(`sdaslh5801` from [sdcc-pc1500](https://github.com/pchambre/sdcc-pc1500)).

The disassemblies are generated by a separate, private annotation project (`pc1600/`);
its symbol tables (`notes/<ROM>-Symbols.md`) and per-image findings
(`notes/<ROM>-Disassembly.md`) are not published. Lines in the `.asm` headers that refer
to `tools/romdis/…`, `.ann` files or `notes/…` belong to that project.

## Provenance

The ROMs were dumped from real PC-1600 / CE-1600P units over COM1:, with a 16-bit sum
computed on the calculator and checked on receipt for every page. The LH5803's private
ROM, which the Z-80 cannot address, was read through the `CALLH` bridge by a small
program running on the LH5803 itself. Earlier versions of this corpus used images taken
from an emulator's source tree; those are no longer the reference. For the NEW revision
they turned out byte-identical to the hardware dumps (see `PC-1600-ROM-Versions.md`).

## Revisions

Two calculator ROM revisions exist; `PRINT PEEK #(0,&7FFF)` gives 5 on NEW and 130 on OLD
(a NEW variant reporting 4 has not been dumped yet). The CE-1600P has two versions of its
own, independent of the calculator's (`PEEK #(5,&7FFE)` = 5 NEW, 4 OLD). See
`PC-1600-ROM-Versions.md` and `PC-1600-ROM/disasm/README.md`.

## Relationship to this corpus

This sub-corpus explains *why* the address space is shaped the way it is (bank switching,
chip selects, dual-CPU bus); the disassemblies are where the bytes become readable code.
Many facts in this sub-corpus are now checked against, or were derived from, that code.
